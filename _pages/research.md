# Research

The PKU-EMBL Lab develops systematic technologies for resolving microbial "dark matter" (<span lang="zh-CN">微生物“暗物质”</span>): rare, uncultivated, slow-growing, or functionally unknown microorganisms hidden in complex environments.

We focus on extreme environments, especially saline-alkaline ecosystems, and combine multi-omics, AI models, open-source software, and wet-lab validation to turn hidden microbial resources into biological mechanisms and biotechnology.

## Research Directions

- **Microbial dark matter discovery**
   Capture rare genomes, low-abundance taxa, and hidden biosynthetic potential from complex microbial communities.

- **Extreme-environment resource atlas**
   Build genome, gene, protein, and biosynthetic gene cluster resources from saline-alkaline and other high-stress ecosystems.

- **AI-enabled pathway and protein discovery**
   Use knowledge graphs, foundation models, and generative models to predict pathways, proteins, peptides, and interactions.

- **Wet-lab validation and translation**
   Validate computational predictions through cultivation, metabolomics, synthetic reconstruction, and microbial community regulation.

## Software

The lab's official GitHub organization is [PKU-EMBL](https://github.com/PKU-EMBL). The core software project is [BASALT](https://github.com/PKU-EMBL/BASALT), a _Nature Communications_ toolkit for metagenomic binning and refinement. Related software includes [BASALT-Air](https://github.com/PKU-EMBL/BASALT-Air).

## Representative Impact

Research led by **Tenured A/Prof. Ke Yu** has produced **more than 90 SCI publications** with **over 5,000 citations**, including papers in _Nature Communications_, _Microbiome_, _Environmental Science & Technology_, and _Water Research_.
