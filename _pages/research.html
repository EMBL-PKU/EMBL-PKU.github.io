Research

The PKU-EMBL Lab develops systematic technologies for resolving microbial "dark matter" (微生物“暗物质”): rare, uncultivated, slow-growing, or functionally unknown microorganisms hidden in complex environments.

We focus on extreme environments, especially saline-alkaline ecosystems, and combine multi-omics, AI models, open-source software, and wet-lab validation to turn hidden microbial resources into biological mechanisms and biotechnology.

Technical Roadmap

A dry-wet integrated route from extreme habitats and multi-omics data to method development and biological application.

Research Directions

  • Microbial dark matter discovery
    Capture rare genomes, low-abundance taxa, and hidden biosynthetic potential from complex microbial communities.
  • Extreme-environment resource atlas
    Build genome, gene, protein, and biosynthetic gene cluster resources from saline-alkaline and other high-stress ecosystems.
  • AI-enabled pathway and protein discovery
    Use knowledge graphs, foundation models, and generative models to predict pathways, proteins, peptides, and interactions.
  • Wet-lab validation and translation
    Validate computational predictions through cultivation, metabolomics, synthetic reconstruction, and microbial community regulation.

Software

The lab's official GitHub organization is PKU-EMBL. The core software project is BASALT, a Nature Communications toolkit for metagenomic binning and refinement. Related software includes BASALT-Air.

Representative Impact

Research led by Tenured A/Prof. Ke Yu includes more than 100 SCI-indexed papers and over 6,000 citations, with first-author or corresponding-author publications in broad-interest, microbiology, environmental science, and environmental engineering journals including Nature Communications, Microbiome, Environmental Science & Technology, and Water Research. His first-author metatranscriptomics study on activated sludge microbiomes was recognized by Prof. Per H. Nielsen as one of the landmark methodological studies for understanding activated-sludge microbial ecology over the past five decades. Another multi-omics study was highlighted by Prof. Marvin Whiteley for advancing methods to study interspecies microbial interactions.
